# Changelog

What changed in Lotus Altar, newest first. Each push to `main` deploys to Vercel, so dates stand in for versions.
For working notes and open questions, see `CONTINUUM.md`.

## 2026-11-26
- The sky outside now has four looks: dawn, day, dusk and night. At sunrise the eastern sky turns peach and rose under a pale violet, at sunset the western sky glows orange into magenta and deep indigo, with a low orange sun and gold light on the temple. The sky button cycles through Dawn, Day, Dusk and Night for this visit, then back to the real sky; a chosen dawn or dusk puts the sun where it stands at today's sunrise or sunset, and the moon stays where it really is.

## 2026-11-25
- The night sky outside, from the pagoda's top floor and the bell tower now holds real stars where they really stand over the temple: Orion, the Plough, Cassiopeia, the Pleiades, Scorpius and Sagittarius, the Southern Cross low in the south, with thousands of fainter stars, and the Milky Way across the sky, brightest toward Sagittarius and split by the Great Rift. The sky turns through the night; the stars twinkle (held still under reduced motion), dim and redden near the horizon, hide behind cloud, rain, mist and haze, and a bright moon washes out the faint ones and the Milky Way.

### Changed
- The high school's windows go dark at 8 PM, when the last teachers and pupils have gone home, and stay dark until 6 AM.
- The festival ground on the stupa hill is lit at night while the shows are on: bulbs along the pavilion's eaves, lamps under its roof, a spotlight on the dancers and warm light over the crowd. The lights come on after dark and go out at 11 PM when the closing dance is over.
- Every night balloon flight looks different: the painted balloons come up in a new colour scheme each time (now and then in their own painted colours), and each fireworks balloon carries its own few kinds of shell and often its own few colours. New shells join the peonies, willows and rings: crossed double rings, saturns (a ball inside a wide ring), palms, crossettes whose stars split again, and twinkling glitter.
- The seven-day market at night: a paper lantern glows under every stall, and warm light over the aisles shows the stalls, their goods and the shoppers.
- The night outside feels like night: moonlit blue shadows, a deep blue sky with the hills dark against it, and the lamps, lanterns and lit windows standing out. The halls and the daytime look are unchanged.

### Added
- Christmas and the harvest thanksgiving at the Kachin Baptist church. On the last Sunday of November the families' first fruits (rice sheaves, sacks of new rice, pumpkins, baskets of fruit and vegetables) lie before the pulpit. From 1 December to 6 January the church is dressed for Christmas: a gold star and coloured lights outside, a tree, a nativity, paper stars and a Jinghpaw greeting inside, and star poles with lights in the yards of the Christian homes beside it. From 1 to 24 December, 7 to 11 PM, carol singers go round the lanes behind a star on a pole and stop to sing at the doors. There are services on Christmas Eve and Christmas morning.

## 2026-11-24

### Added
- Shan Kathin, the offering of robes after the rains retreat. On one day, the full moon of Tazaungmon (Tazaungdaing) in November, the town carries one new set of monastic robes from Haw Kham around the old quarter to the second monastery at noon, led by a great yellow flag, Buddhist flags and strings of little flags, with five padetha trees (one hung with banknotes under a white umbrella), a tower of flowers, a palanquin and the Shan band, and five monks wait on the porch to receive them. They have gone home by 2 PM, and that morning there is no Shinlaung procession. It is called off while it rains. On this computer, `?kathin=1` shows it at any time.

### Changed
- The fireworks from the night balloons are sharp now instead of blurry: each star is a small white-hot point with a streak behind it, and the shells burst as coloured balls, gold willows that droop, or rings, some ending in a crackle of glitter.
