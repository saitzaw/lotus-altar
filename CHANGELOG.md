# Changelog

What changed in Lotus Altar, newest first. Each push to `main` deploys to Vercel, so dates stand in for versions.
For working notes and open questions, see `CONTINUUM.md`.

## 2026-11-10

### Added
- `SITEMAP.md` (every place, how to reach it, the code that builds it, performance test routes) and `ARCHITECTURE.md`
  (load sequence, frame loop, likely hotspots, a plan for a `?perf=1` overlay and field tracking, tuning ideas).

## 2026-11-09

### Added
- The to dance (Shan တၢင်းၵႃႈတူဝ်း, Burmese တိုးနယား) on the festival stage from 6 to 7:30 PM Bangkok time: two dancers in one
  white beast costume trimmed in gold, green and red prance, bow to the crowd, rear up, crouch, shake the mane and wag the tail
  to the Shan ensemble. It has a deer's face and branched antlers.
- A second, yellow to, which dances with the white one, and a closing dance with both to and the kinnara.
- The festival ground's pavilion stage is wider and deeper (14 × 6.5 m), so the dancers have room; the band, the flag pole and
  the audience moved back to suit.

### Changed
- The festival stage's programme (Bangkok time): the kinnara alone 2–3:30 PM, the kinnara and kinnari 4–5:45 PM, the to 6–7:30 PM,
  the two to 7:45–8:30 PM, the kinnara and kinnari 8:45–9:45 PM, and the two to with the kinnara 10–11 PM.
- The front street outside the gate has the town road's tall modern street lights in place of the red lantern posts, and
  they now run its whole length, from the hospital corner to the police station.
- When it rains during a kinnara dance, the children in the festival ground's front rows go home and the young people standing
  behind stay to watch under umbrellas; the dancers and the band are dry under the pavilion roof.

## 2026-11-07

### Changed
- The festival stage's Shan ensemble looks and sounds more like the real thing: the five gongs hang in a painted Shan gong case
  facing the crowd, and one player on a stool at its end pushes a rod that strikes all five at once, two drummers each play a long drum slung from the shoulder and skirted in yellow, green and red, a cymbal player keeps the
  beat again, and the drum, gongs and cymbals play a quicker three-beat rhythm voiced after a recording of a Shan ensemble.

### Fixed
- The site no longer goes black from 4 to 11 PM (Bangkok time) while the kinnara pair dance on the festival stage.

### Added
- A row of five graduated gongs (မွင်း) joins the long drummer on the festival stage, played by one musician who turns from gong
  to gong; you can hear the drum and gongs near the festival ground. The kinnara lands on the drum and the big gong; in the evening
  the pair open and fold their wings gong to gong. The stage's labels use Shan names.
- A Shan long-drum player (kawng yao) on the festival stage, keeping the beat for the kinnara dancers; you can hear the drum
  near the festival ground.

### Changed
- The kinnara's solo is gentler: gliding steps and slowly opening wings, with only a light hop now and then.
- The long drum sounds more like a kawng yao: a deep booming centre stroke, a ringing open stroke and a muffled slap.
- The kinnara's solo is slower: a leap to each bar of the drum, with time to land, settle and gather between leaps.
- The kinnara dancer now dances with his arms and legs: knees that bend to land and spring, a leg kicked back in each leap,
  and curled-back dancer's hands. His leaps rise and fall naturally instead of floating.
- Walking is gentler on the eyes: looking round with the mouse or a finger is slower and stops when you let go, arrow keys turn
  more slowly, and the view on foot no longer widens to a fish-eye on phones.

### Added
- Dragon and lion dance training on the kung fu ground in the 30 days before Chinese New Year: the dragon from 6 to 9 AM and 9 to 10 PM,
  the lions from 9:30 to 11 AM and 6 to 8 PM (Bangkok time), with a drummer and cymbal player.

## 2026-11-03

### Added
- The Shan monastery's shrine hall opens, with a Shan-style Buddha; its stair joins the hill stair.
- A paved plinth lets walkers circle the Shan monastery.
- The stupa and monastery carry name boards in Shan.
- Visitors can step inside the Kachin Baptist church.

- Five two-storey Shan houses facing the Haw Kham.
- The street runs on west and north to the hospital's forecourt.
- A small township football ground across the road from the hospital.

### Changed
- Every lamp goes out by day, and daylight comes into the halls.
- The stupa hill's entrance gate is raised so its name board stands over the stair.
- The Shan image hall is dressed the Shan way: red-and-gold back wall, blue halo, gilt altar. Offerings stand on the
  throne's ledge instead of a table.
- The Shan Buddha's head is bare: dark curls and a small gilt bud, no jewelled band.
- The night sky is black.
- The title and location line no longer show at the top of the screen.

### Fixed
- The gap at the top of the monastery stair is closed, so walkers can step onto the verandah.
- The monastery verandah's back railing has balusters like its sides and front.
- The festival ground's flag pole no longer crowds the pavilion steps.

### Removed
- Trees and shrubs on the path to the main stupa.
- The foothill that stood across the road to the hospital.
